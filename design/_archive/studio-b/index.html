Studio BTurnout · identity, final sheet19 October 2026

The name is already a gesture.

Turnout is a verb before it is a noun: the moment a crowd turns, out of the arena, round the corner, through somebody's door. The sheets so far drew that moment as a picture inside a 32 pixel circle, and drew it well. We think it belongs in the letters. Turnout will be read in a header beside a serif, on a receipt, on a lock screen at 22:41, by owners who run good rooms and have no patience for software that looks like software; it should feel like a house that has been on the corner for forty years, and it should carry its idea in the type people already read. So we started from Fraunces itself, its leaning n and its hairline T, and from the pavement outside a venue at the hour the doors open. Three ideas follow. One is pure typography and risks being wrong. One is the radar you liked, given a house to live in. One opens a door in the middle of the name.

01

The Turn

Typography. The bold one.

The n of Fraunces and the same n turned half a revolution, joined through its leaning stem: an arch standing on the street line, a cup hanging below it. The arch is the arena, the cup is the glass, and Turnout is the turn between them, drawn from the wordmark's own letter so the mark and the name speak with one voice.

It is the only mark of the three that survives the gesture it names: turn it upside down and it is still Turnout. It needs no picture of a crowd, no screen, no icon; a typographer would recognise the n in a second, and everyone else sees a line that goes in and comes out warm.

Where it failsRead left to right it spells "nu", French for naked, and someone will make that joke once. It carries no radar, so the product's first screen has to do that job.

02

The Seal

The radar, given a house.

A brasserie seal, the kind pressed into a menu or the back of a matchbook, whose inner field is a working radar: rings, one crisp leading edge, and an afterglow that runs on into the lettering, so the letters the sweep has just passed are still warm. It reads TURN in ink and OUT in amber, because that is what the radar just saw.

It keeps everything you liked in the radar sheets and gives it a house: premium, hospitable, something an owner would frame behind the bar. Below 96 px it sheds its words and becomes the plain radar, so the small sizes are already proven.

Where it failsIt is the most expected of the three: seals are a known hospitality device, and at 24 px it is one more radar icon among many.

03

The Opening

Negative space and light.

Turnout is two words, turn and out; we open a door between them. The baseline becomes a facade seen from above, it breaks where the door is, and the afterglow becomes what it always wanted to be: warm light falling through an open door onto the pavement. Cropped, that doorway is a T: the crossbar is the wall, the stem is the light.

It is the only idea that shows outside and inside at once, and it hands the brand a layout gesture (one line, one opening, one pool of light) that scales from a favicon to every campaign the agent publishes.

Where it failsSplitting the name is a real risk: some will read "Turn out" as an instruction, and alone at 24 px the T of light can look like a T running out of ink.

We recommend The Turn.

It is the one idea here that could only belong to Turnout. The arch and the cup are the same letter, the letter is from the wordmark, and the mark survives the very gesture it names. It is calm at 20 px beside the serif, it is a glyph rather than a pictogram, and it gives the brand a story an owner can repeat across the bar: the arena and the glass are the same shape, turned.

Keep the radar where it is strongest: as the product's first screen and its loading state, drawn exactly as the sixth sheet left it. The Turn is what Turnout means; the radar is what it does. If you need the radar in the logo itself, The Seal is the fallback and it is ready. The Opening is the gesture we would keep either way, as the layout device for campaigns.

Construction. The two halves meet on the n's own baseline, so the mark has a street line through its middle: outside above, inside below. Ink is the crowd, amber is the room.

The one risk

There is no radar in it. A founder, an investor or a juror who expects to see the product in the logo will not find it; the mark asks them to read a letter instead of a picture. We think that is the point, and the product's first screen answers them in one second.

Studio B for Turnout. Every mark is SVG geometry. The wordmark and The Turn are Fraunces outlines (Google Fonts build, wght 500, opsz 144 and 24), read back from the font as paths so the mark and the word come from the same drawing; the seal lettering is Fraunces 600 at opsz 36, the descriptor Inter 600 at 0.14 em. Afterglows are baked, solid slices; the light of The Opening is one linear gradient. No raster anywhere in the marks.
