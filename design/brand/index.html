Turnout  ·  brand guidelines  ·  v1.0, October 2026

Turn the crowd outside into customers inside.

Turnout is an agent for independent bars, restaurants and shops near venues. It sees a crowd coming, learns what that crowd loves, picks the offer, writes the campaign and publishes it once the owner says yes. The brand has to feel like both halves of that sentence: the warmth of a good room, and the precision of a tool that is right about people.

01  Brand idea

One paragraph the whole team can repeat, and three principles that decide every visual call.

Turnout. Turn the crowd outside into customers inside. We are the host who already knows what the people walking past will love, and who says so plainly. Hospitality warmth, data precision. Paper, ink and one amber light. Never dark, never neon, never a dashboard pretending to be a nightclub.

Warm paper, not dark glass

The product lives on paper #F7F4EE with white panels. Ink is for text and primary actions. The owner rejected dark and "geek" aesthetics; every screen should look fine printed.

One amber

Amber #B4530A is the person who showed up, the thing to look at, the one decision on the page. One accent per view. If two things are amber, neither is.

Evidence, not promises

We say what the taste graph shows and what it does not establish. Numbers are tabular, sources are visible, verbs are careful. We never say "they will come".

Rules

  • Amber is scarce: one accent per view. The score ring, the one call to action, the lit dot. Not all three.
  • Ink is the primary action. .btn-primary is ink; .btn-accent is reserved for the single moment of approval ("Publish").
  • Amber text on paper or white: use --accent-text below 18 px. --accent itself is AA on paper (4.57) but fails on amber-soft; never set amber text on amber-soft below 14 px bold.
  • Muted text (--muted) is the floor for anything the user must read. --faint is for disabled states and large labels only.
  • Semantic colours speak once, in a badge or a left border. No coloured backgrounds on whole cards.
  • Reverse surfaces (ink) are for physical objects and previews: chalkboards, OG images, tooltips. Never a whole screen.

Map styling

  • Tiles: OSM or Carto light, desaturated with filter: saturate(0.55) contrast(0.95), 14 px radius, 1 px line border. The map should look like it was printed on the page.
  • Merchant: ink dot 16 px, 2 px paper ring, 1 px line-strong halo. Always the darkest thing on the map.
  • Hot venues (event in window): amber dot 14 px, same rings. Label on a white chip with line-strong border, Inter 600 11 px.
  • Cold venues: ink-300 dot, muted label. Present, so the merchant sees what the radar watches, but quiet.
  • Radius: 1.5 px dashed line-strong circle, no fill. Label bottom right, muted, "600 m radius".
  • No heatmaps, no pulsing circles, no blue. Concentration is shown in the list beside the map, not painted on it.

07  Iconography

A 20 px grid, 1.75 px stroke, round caps and joins, 1.5 px inner padding, no fills except the one amber dot where an icon needs a focal point. Drawn to sit beside Inter 14 px at 20 px, and beside 13 px at 16 px (same stroke, simpler geometry).

  • Stroke 1.75 px at 20 px (8.75 %), round caps and joins, vector-effect off: icons scale with their stroke.
  • Geometry on the half pixel for strokes, whole pixels for fills. Circles with overshoot: r 7 inside an 18 px safe area.
  • Colour: currentColor, inherits text colour. The only fill allowed is one amber dot, r 1, for a focal point (offer, listing).
  • No megaphones, no pins, no radar sweeps. "Publish" is an arrow through a door; "crowd" is heads, not a people pictogram.
  • Icons sit on the text baseline, vertically centred on the x-height of Inter: 20 px icon beside 14 px text, 16 px beside 12 and 13 px.
  • Icon plus label always; icon-only buttons need a tooltip and an aria-label.
  • Third-party brands (WhatsApp, Google) are never redrawn. Use the neutral "message" and "listing" icons with the channel name in text.
  • New icons are drawn, not downloaded. If a library is needed, Lucide at 1.75 px matches; adapt caps to round.

08  Voice and copy

Short, warm, direct. We write like a good host who has done the homework: specific, calm, never excited on the merchant's behalf.

  • Sentences under 18 words. One idea each. Verbs first in buttons ("Review campaign", "Publish", "Not now").
  • No exclamation marks, no emojis, no hashtags, no "Boost your sales" with a party popper. Enthusiasm is shown by precision.
  • Numbers are specific and sourced: "19 000 ticket holders, doors 18:30", not "a huge crowd".
  • We say "you" to the merchant, "they" for the crowd, "we" for Turnout. Never "our AI".
  • Time is written 18:30, dates 18 Oct, distances 600 m. Money in the merchant's currency with the symbol they use.
  • Errors say what happened and what to do next, in that order. No apologies longer than two words.

Talking about Qloo evidence

Don't

Hozier fans will flood your bar looking for natural wine tonight!

Our AI predicts a 40 % uplift.

These people love you.

Do

Fans of Hozier over-index on natural wine (11x the city baseline, Qloo).

Tonight, 19 000 of them pass within 600 m between 17:45 and 20:30.

This does not establish how many will stop.

The verb is always "over-index", "are more likely to", "tend to". Never "will come", "love you", "are your customers". The caution box is part of the voice, not a legal footer.

10  Motion

Motion confirms, it never decorates. Three durations, two curves, a short list of things that move and a longer list of things that do not.

dur-1
120 ms

Hover and press: button background, card lift of 1 px, chip selection. ease-out.

dur-2
200 ms

Reveal and state: badge colour change, expand a provenance row, open a menu. ease-out in, ease-in-out for height.

dur-3
320 ms

The two moments that deserve it: the score ring filling on first paint, and the publish confirmation. ease-out.

Publish confirmation

→Published to Google listing, 16:00

The accent button becomes a line of text with a check that draws in over 320 ms (stroke-dashoffset). No confetti, no toast sliding in from a corner. The badge on the card changes to "Published" at the same time (200 ms).

--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1) · --ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)

Never animates

  • Page backgrounds, gradients, the paper. Nothing breathes.
  • The logo. No spinning door, no dots walking in. The mark is still.
  • Table rows on load, cards staggering in, numbers counting up. Data appears, it does not perform.
  • Anything that loops, except the 7 px dot of "Agent working" at 1.2 s, which stops under reduced motion.
  • Map markers. No pulsing radius, no ripples. The radar is a list, not a sonar.

Focus ring

Vinyl shops

:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--accent); outline-offset: 2px; }
.field:focus { border-color: var(--accent); box-shadow: 0 0 0 3px var(--accent-soft); }

Amber is the only focus colour, on every element, including links and table rows. Never outline: none without a replacement of equal visibility.

Target sizes

4044chip 26 px, needs 8 px gap

40 x 40 px minimum for pointer, 44 x 44 on touch layouts (under 768 px). Small buttons (30 px tall) get invisible padding to 40. Chips are not targets unless they remove or toggle, in which case they are 32 px tall.

Reduced motion and other rules

  • prefers-reduced-motion: reduce sets all three durations to 0 and stops the working-dot pulse. The ring renders filled.
  • Status is never colour alone: every badge has a word, every ring has a number, every hot venue has a label.
  • Fields have visible labels; placeholders are examples, not labels.
  • Fraunces italic amber is decoration: screen readers get the same sentence without emphasis markup unless it changes meaning.
  • Language attribute follows the merchant's locale (lang="fr" for France) so dates and numbers are read correctly.

12  Changes from the current app

Everything here keeps the existing variable names in globals.css. These are the differences, and why.

  • Added --accent-text #A54A07. --accent stays #B4530A for fills and the mark; it is AA on paper (4.57) but 4.25 on amber-soft. Amber text under 18 px uses accent-text.
  • Added --warn-text #805300, --ok-text #1B6B44. --warn #9A6400 is 4.56 on paper; fine for borders, marginal for 12 px badge text. The text variants pass with room.
  • Added --info / --info-soft (#3B5B7A / #E6EDF3). The app had no colour for "agent working"; a slate, so it never reads as SaaS blue.
  • Added the ink scale (ink-50 to ink-950), --faint, --placeholder. The app used #a39c8f and #2b2820 as literals; they are now tokens, and the hover ink is --ink-900.
  • Added --accent-hover #9D4808, which the app had as a literal in .btn-accent:hover.
  • Added --radius-sm 8, --radius-field 10, --radius-pill 999. Fields already used 10 px as a literal.
  • Added --shadow-lift and --shadow-pop for hover and menus; --shadow unchanged.
  • Added data-viz tokens (--seq-1..5, --neu-1..5, --map-*) and the type scale, spacing, motion and focus tokens. None existed.
  • Logo: the mark in Logo.tsx changes to direction 01 (open side on the left, solid dots, no opacity), the wordmark gains font-variation-settings: "opsz" 144 and letter-spacing -0.02 em (was -0.015 to -0.02), and the header uses the mark at 20 px tall beside the 20 px wordmark (was 1.45 x font size). public/logo.svg and src/app/icon.svg are replaced by design/logo/01-threshold.svg and design/brand/icon.svg.
  • Unchanged: paper, panel, panel-2, line, line-strong, ink, text, muted, accent, accent-ink, accent-soft, ok, ok-soft, warn, warn-soft, danger, danger-soft, radius, shadow.
