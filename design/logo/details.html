Ramps, lockups, grids.

Everything behind each direction: size ramps on paper and white, horizontal and stacked lockups, reverse, one colour, and the construction on the 32 unit grid with the 16 px drawing beside the 32 px one.

01The threshold

A squared doorway open on the reading side. Three people stand on the floor line and grow as they come in. The amber one has just crossed.

Pros

  • The approved idea, finished.
  • Square corners stay pixel-crisp; survives 16 px with one dot fewer.
  • Floor and baseline are the same line.

Cons

  • Busiest of the six.
  • Growing dots are a known device; the frame carries the meaning.

02The turn

A single path comes in from the left, turns the corner and arrives at an amber point. The moment the crowd changes course toward you.

Pros

  • Quietest and most elegant; nothing to clog.
  • Says the name: turn.
  • Also works as an icon language (routes, arrows) in the product.

Cons

  • A line ending in a dot can read as a route or a cursor.
  • The crowd is implied, not shown.

03The count

A tally. Four ink strokes, and the fifth, the one that completes the count, in amber. Turnout as something you can count.

Pros

  • Immediate: everyone knows a tally.
  • Speaks to the data side without a chart.
  • Monochrome version is the strongest of the six.

Cons

  • Tally marks carry prison and bar-tab jokes.
  • No place in it: nothing says hospitality.

04The lit window

A facade at night. Five windows and a door cut out of solid ink; one window is lit amber. The place the crowd is drawn to.

Pros

  • Warm, narrative, instantly a place.
  • Solid shape: the strongest favicon and app icon.
  • Amber means light, which is what amber is for.

Cons

  • Heavy next to a light paper UI; use small.
  • At 20 px the windows become texture.
  • House icons are everywhere; the night and the one light must carry it.

05The queue becomes a crowd

A queue of dots, spaced out, that gathers into a cluster. The amber dot is the centre everyone ends up around.

Pros

  • Tells the whole story, arrival and gathering.
  • Clean dot language shared with the product's charts.
  • Unique silhouette.

Cons

  • Hexagonal cluster reads molecule or network at mid sizes.
  • Wide format: weak in the header, weak as a favicon.
  • No place, only people.

06Under the n

The wordmark itself. Fraunces 500 at -0.02 em, and one amber dot standing under the arch of the n, the way a person stands in a doorway. The n alone is the monogram.

Pros

  • Nothing to add to the wordmark; the brand is the name.
  • The most editorial, the least 'app'.
  • Monogram and wordmark share one gesture.

Cons

  • The dot can read as a blot or a typo.
  • Depends on the font rendering: fragile in email signatures and partner decks.
  • At 20 px the dot nearly disappears.

07The sweep

A quarter-circle sector in the soft tint, its outer edge in ink, over a field of venue dots. The dot the sweep has reached is lit in accent.

Pros

  • Reads radar without a sonar screen.
  • The tint gives the mark warmth and a second surface.
  • Clean at 20 px in the header.

Cons

  • Needs the soft tint: three colours instead of two.
  • Field dots are decoration at small sizes.

08Rings and a door

One dashed ring, the radius. At its centre, the doorway glyph: the merchant. Two dots on the ring, and the accent dot already inside it, on its way to the door.

Pros

  • Literally the radar screen: radius, merchant, crowd.
  • Keeps the doorway from direction 01 in the family.
  • Beautiful at 48 px and above.

Cons

  • Dashes and the door both fail at 16 px; the favicon is a different drawing.
  • At 20 px the door is 5 px wide: it reads as a block.

09The map

A street corner from above: three solid blocks around a crossing, the dashed radius over them, and the accent dot where the fourth block would be. Your corner of the city.

Pros

  • Concrete: a map, a radius, a place.
  • Solid shapes, the easiest of the five to keep crisp.
  • Pairs naturally with the radar screen.

Cons

  • Three dark blocks are heavy beside a light serif wordmark.
  • Could read as a window or a QR fragment at 24 px.

10Signal

An ink point emitting two accent arcs to the right. A beacon: the merchant sending a signal to the crowd. The simplest possible radar.

Pros

  • Nothing to simplify; crisp at every size.
  • Fast to read, calm, professional.
  • Works as a status icon inside the product (listening, published).

Cons

  • Generic: wifi, sonar, podcast marks live here.
  • No place and no crowd, only the signal.

11Venue and bar

A large light circle, the arena. A small ink square, the bar. A dotted path between them with the accent dot in motion: the crowd on its way.

Pros

  • Tells the exact product story: from there to here.
  • Two shapes and a dot: easy to animate in onboarding, still as a mark.
  • Light and professional.

Cons

  • Abstract until explained: circle, square, dot.
  • Diagonal marks sit less well on a baseline.

12Classic

Two concentric rings in ink, a quarter sweep as an 18 % tint of the accent from the centre, the leading edge of the sweep in ink, and one accent blip just inside that edge. The centre is a small ink dot.

Pros

  • Reads radar at a glance.
  • Tint plus one blip is calm, not military.
  • Palette swap is one token.

Cons

  • Thin rings are 0.9 px at 16 px: soft.
  • The most literal of the five; the least ownable.

13Rings and door

Two rings. At the centre, the doorway glyph from direction 01, reduced to a bracket. One accent blip sitting on the outer ring. The merchant at the centre of its radius.

Pros

  • Keeps the doorway in the family.
  • The blip on the ring reads 'detected at the edge of my radius'.
  • Still clearly a radar.

Cons

  • The bracket at 20 px is a 6 px glyph: a block with a gap.
  • Two ideas in one mark.

14Half radar

The upper half of a radar: a horizon line, two concentric semicircular arcs rising from it, the centre dot on the horizon, one accent blip between the arcs.

Pros

  • Best header fit of the five.
  • A radar screen, not a target.
  • Quiet, almost typographic.

Cons

  • Wide marks are weak stacked and as app icons.
  • Can read as a sunrise or a speedometer.

15Sweep only

A centre dot, one long quarter sweep in ink, a shorter inner arc, and the accent blip at the end of the sweep. The quiet one.

Pros

  • Calmest; nothing to clog.
  • The blip 'arrives' where the sweep stops: a small story.
  • Family resemblance to 02 The turn.

Cons

  • Needs the ring context to be read as radar.
  • Open composition sits low on the baseline.

16Radar in the o

Turnout, with the first o replaced by a radar: a heavier ring at the o's size, a tiny sweep tint and one blip. The standalone o is the favicon.

Pros

  • The word is the mark; nothing to place beside it.
  • The favicon is literally the o.
  • Playful without a mascot.

Cons

  • Mixes a monoline ring with a high-contrast serif.
  • Depends on font metrics; fragile outside the web app.
  • Blip disappears at 20 px.

17Dial

Outer ring 1.4 units, inner ring 1.0, the sweep's leading edge as a 0.8 hairline, tint at 12 %, blip r 1.6 just behind the edge, a 1.2 centre dot. The precision of a watch dial.

Pros

  • Most 'instrument' of the four.
  • Hairline edge gives the sweep direction without a spoke.

Cons

  • Hierarchy flattens at 20 px.
  • Hairline disappears in print under 12 mm.

18Weighted

Clear hierarchy: outer ring 1.8, inner ring 1.0, no edge line, the sweep as tint only, the blip r 1.8 sitting exactly on the inner ring, no centre dot. The eye lands on the blip.

Pros

  • Calmest; the fewest elements that still say radar.
  • Hierarchy survives the header.

Cons

  • Without a centre the radar can read as a target or a coin.
  • Blip on the ring can read as a planet on an orbit.

19Open ring

A single outer ring with a gap where the sweep is: the ring is interrupted by the sector. Inner ring as a hairline, the blip in the gap, a small centre. The opening says door without drawing one.

Pros

  • The gap is a door and a sweep at once: the one idea of the brand in the radar.
  • Reads at 16 px without losing the opening.

Cons

  • An open ring can read as a loading spinner.
  • The blip in the gap sits outside the sweep's band, so it is 'arriving' rather than 'detected'.

20Lens

Two concentric rings with a third, very faint one. The sweep is a soft tint graded from 0 to 18 % (hero only; flat 14 % at small sizes). The blip sits slightly outward so it is not dead-centred in the sector. The centre is empty.

Pros

  • Most refined at 96 px; the gradient reads as light, not paint.
  • Empty centre keeps it from being a target.

Cons

  • The faint ring is invisible below 48 px.
  • Gradient needs a defs id per instance: fine in SVG, awkward in CSS backgrounds.

21Afterglow

Rings r 14 at 1.4 and r 8.5 at 0.9, even weight. The sweep is a 90 degree angular gradient trail ending at the leading edge, opacity from 0 at the tail to 0.28 at the edge, built as 14 stacked sectors. Blip r 1.7 at 43 degrees from vertical on the inner ring. No centre, no edge line.

Pros

  • A real radar: the glow has direction and time.
  • Still two rings and one dot.

Cons

  • Steps need 14 paths; fine in SVG, not in CSS without a conic gradient.
  • Glow is invisible in print under 10 mm.

22Afterglow, long

Same rings. The trail is 140 degrees at a maximum of 0.22, with a 0.6 hairline leading edge in accent at 60 % opacity. Blip at 43 degrees. The long trail gives the feel of a screen.

Pros

  • The most 'live' of the three.
  • The hairline edge gives the sweep a now.

Cons

  • At 24 px the hairline edge is 0.45 px: gone, honestly.
  • Longest trail, most pixels of tint on a light UI.

23Afterglow, light rings

Rings r 14 at 1.2 and r 8.5 at 0.8, both in the softer ink (ink at 85 %, token --mark-ink-soft). Trail 110 degrees at max 0.26. Blip r 1.8. The glow carries the mark.

Pros

  • Lightest, most editorial.
  • The glow becomes the subject.

Cons

  • Soft rings on paper lose 0.4 units of presence; on white they are fine.
  • Needs the full-ink exception at 16 px.
