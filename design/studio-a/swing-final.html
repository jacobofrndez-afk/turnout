Swing, sharpened.

Same idea, three finesses. What changed in every execution: the door is now one construction rather than four drawings. The leaf is exactly as long as the doorway is wide, it ends on the arc's inner edge, the arc's inner edge drops straight into the face of the right wall, and both walls are the same length and sit on the hinge line. The founder judges elegance at 256 px; we made sure the 16 and 20 px masters keep the same drawing pixel for pixel.

The construction, S1 on the 32 unit grid

Precision rules, met in all three

  1. Hinge on a whole unit: (6, 26). Nothing is drawn there: the hinge is where the lines meet.
  2. Arc centred on the hinge. Its inner edge is the whole number, r 20; the centreline is r 20 + arc/2 (20.5 in S1). It starts on the hinge line and ends exactly above the hinge, at x 6.
  3. Leaf drawn as a closed shape, not a stroke: its base is cut on the hinge line (no sliver below the wall line, ever), its top is an arc on r 20, so it ends exactly on the arc's inner edge and never crosses it. Leaf length = door width = 20.
  4. Walls: M2 26H6 and M26 26H30, butt caps, both 4 units, both centred on the hinge line, outer ends on whole units (2 and 30, symmetric in the 32 box). The left wall stops on the hinge; the right wall starts on the arc's inner edge, so the arc lands on it flush.
  5. Afterglow clipped by construction: sectors of radius 20 (the arc's inner edge) from the hinge line to the leaf. It never touches the arc stroke or goes below the wall line.
  6. Pixel masters: at 16 px (1 px = 2 units) the walls are 2 units on y 26..28, x 2..6 and 26..30. At 20 px no 32 unit grid can be crisp, so the 20 px master is drawn natively on a 20 unit grid: walls 1 px on row 16..17, x 1..4 and 16..19. To centre a 1 px wall, the 20 px hinge sits on a pixel centre, (4, 16.5): x whole, y half.

Our pick

S1, Fine. Thin enough to be elegant, strong enough for the header.

At 256 px S1 has the same weight as the hairlines of the Fraunces wordmark: arc 1.0, leaf 1.2, walls 1.4. The walls read as finished wall ends, the leaf is the only colour, and the afterglow at 0.22 is a glow rather than a fill.

S2 is the most beautiful above 128 px, but at 20 px its arc turns grey. S3 is the safest at 20 px and the heaviest at 256. S1's 20 px master gets S3's safety anyway: walls 1 px, arc 0.94 px, so the header never pays for the elegance of the large mark.

export function TurnoutMark({ size = 32, title }: { size?: number; title?: string }) {
  const m = size <= 18 ? SWING.px16 : size <= 22 ? SWING.px20 : SWING.main;
  return (
    <svg width={size} height={size} viewBox={`0 0 ${m.vb} ${m.vb}`} fill="none" role={title ? "img" : undefined} aria-hidden={title ? undefined : true}>
      {title && <title>{title}</title>}
      {m.glow.map(([d, o]) => <path key={d} d={d} fill="var(--accent)" fillOpacity={o} />)}
      {m.ring && <path d={m.ring[0]} stroke="var(--ink)" strokeWidth={m.ring[1]} strokeOpacity={m.ring[2]} />}
      <path d={m.arc[0]} stroke="var(--ink)" strokeWidth={m.arc[1]} />
      <path d={m.stubs[0]} stroke="var(--ink)" strokeWidth={m.stubs[1]} strokeLinecap="butt" />
      <path d={m.leaf} fill="var(--accent)" />
    </svg>
  );
}
